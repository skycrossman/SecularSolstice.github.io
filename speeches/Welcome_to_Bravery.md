# Welcome to Bravery
## By Skyler Crossman

Hello!

Welcome to Winter Solstice. We are gathered here tonight on the 7th longest night in Boston, to shine a little light - somewhat literal, but mostly metaphorical - in the darkness.

Some of you might have been coming here for years. Some of you might be here for the very first time. All of you are welcome. Those of you who have been to these before, please shout out what number Solstice this is for you.

…

And those of you who haven't, please shout "What's going on?"

…

I'm so glad you asked. I'm going to be unusually overt about what we're doing.

Holidays and traditions are, among other things, a way to encode and remember virtues a community finds important. Thanksgiving talks about gratitude. Christmas is about charity.

The first step of Solstice was about fourteen billion years ago, which is the current estimate for the origin of the universe. Really, that's the first step of anything if you think about it. The second step of Solstice was about four and a half billion years ago, which is about when the earth formed in its orbit and would begin to have a point when parts of its surface were tilted away from the sun more often, creating shorter days and longer nights. The third step of Solstice was around twelve thousand years ago, which is our current best guess for when religion became a feature of human society. We started telling stories about why it was that things got dark, and cold, and hard. We started traditions.

Some of those stories contained darkness and things that we were afraid of. Our stories were, importantly, usually false. But to paraphrase G. K. Chesterton, fairy tales are important not because they tell us dragons exist, but because they tell us that dragons can be beaten. That courage is a good and valuable thing, and those stories weren't utterly and completely wrong; it really did get colder for a while, then warmer. Tonight we're here to carry on a pattern that's older than writing. We are gathered here as a community to mark the longest night of the year with a ritual that upholds our values.

It's also fun. I'm here because I enjoy it. I hope you do too.

I only want to gently nudge you towards thinking of yourself as one of us. Such things shouldn't be rushed. Eventually you will know whether you are or not.

But I do want you to feel welcome. Even if you're confused. We're all confused, one way or another.

And I do want you to be a part of things tonight. Tonight isn't about those of us on stage doing things to you or for you. It's about you doing things too. We're going to have music tonight, and it's for all of us to sing. Some of you may be anxious or afraid to sing, and if that's you I want you to have courage and sing anyway.

Speaking of courage, those of you who have been coming for a couple of years may have noticed a certain pattern in our themes.

It is a common misconception, thought Harry, that all the best rationalists are Sorted into Ravenclaw, leaving none for other Houses. This is not so; being Sorted into Ravenclaw indicates that your strongest virtue is curiosity, wondering and desiring to know the true answer. And this is not the only virtue a rationalist needs. Sometimes you have to work hard on a problem, and stick to it for a while. Sometimes you need a clever plan for finding out. And sometimes what you need more than anything else to see an answer, is the courage to face it…

Courage comes in all kinds of forms. There's the courage to sing, there's the courage to care and to risk being hurt, there's the courage to defy social norms.

(One word of caution: "that was courageous" is not the same as "that was smart." Do try to keep the difference in mind, even if tonight I am going to assume most of you have "smart" down and could use more courage.)

Speaking of defying social norms, we're going to do something different this Solstice. I am going to deliberately make one lie. It will be in a speech, not a song. It will be a speech I give, not someone else. Each of you gets one chance tonight to try and point it out- to stand up and raise your hand, and say "that's false Skyler." Think of it as a bit of practice in the virtue of courage. If one must be wrong sometimes, it is better to be wrong out loud and get corrected. That's true for both you and me tonight.

I'll start practicing a small bit of courage right now. There's the courage to admit that your deeply held community is kiiiind of sort of if you squint an overgrown Harry Potter Fanfiction club.
